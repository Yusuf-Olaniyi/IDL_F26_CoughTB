# Experiment 6: external test on Zambia and Kenya

## Configuration
* Training: all of CODA (1,082 people with coughs, 291 TB, 9,772 coughs), shared nine questions, folds from coda_folds.csv (seed 42).
* Test sets: Zambia analysis cohort (325 people, 57 TB, 5,287 clips; pooled and per phone A, B, C); Kenya TBscreen passive coughs recorded on the phone ("pixel"), 118 people (84 TB), 1,094 clips, at most 10 per person drawn with a seeded random draw.
* Kenya exclusion: 139 labelled people have pixel passive coughs listed in the metadata, but the audio files of 21 of them (12 TB, 9 non-TB; 209 files) are not in the container. TBscreen released stored VGGish embeddings for them but no raw audio, so they cannot be cut into 0.5 s clips and are left out.
* Every clip: loudest 0.5 s at 16 kHz (`to_clip` of Notebook 04). Representations: mfcc (MFCC summary), spectrogram (spectrogram summary), vggish (pretrained VGGish) and hear (HeAR, `google/hear-pytorch`, frozen; the 0.5 s clip is repeated to 2 s as in Notebook 05, 512 numbers per clip, linear probe only). OPERA to follow.
* Models, threshold rule and seed as in Notebook 04: logistic regression, 90 % sensitivity threshold frozen on out-of-fold predictions of the training people, seed 42. Test labels are used for scoring only.
* Questions: a question is dropped if its missingness in the training data differs by more than 20 points between TB groups. The rule uses training labels only. All nine questions were used in every run (`questions_used` column of e6_results.csv).
* Notebook version: nb06-v3 (HeAR added). The v3 run reproduces every v2 number for MFCC, spectrogram and VGGish (checked in Step 10b of the notebook). An earlier run (nb06-v1) applied the missingness rule to the test set; it dropped nothing, and the v2 run reproduces every number of the v1 run exactly.

## What did you find?
* Train on CODA, test on Zambia and Kenya, MFCC, spectrogram and VGGish: cough audio added nothing measurable to the questions. Zambia (pooled): gain -0.005 (MFCC), -0.016 (spectrogram), +0.002 (VGGish), all intervals include zero; questions alone 0.696. Kenya: +0.006, +0.011, -0.009, intervals about -0.04 to +0.05; questions alone 0.668. Per phone in Zambia the picture is the same; the spectrogram summary is slightly negative on phones A and B (p = 0.03 and 0.015, uncorrected, 15 comparisons).
* **HeAR is different.** Same setup, HeAR as the cough representation: Zambia (pooled) questions 0.696, cough alone 0.719, both 0.727, gain +0.031 (95 % interval +0.010 to +0.053, p = 0.005). Per phone: +0.035 (A, p = 0.003), +0.026 (B, p = 0.026), +0.030 (C, p = 0.025), all intervals above zero. Kenya: questions 0.668, cough alone 0.633, both 0.706, gain +0.038 (-0.005 to +0.082, p = 0.086). The gain is positive in every CODA-trained test set, which no other representation showed. After correcting for multiple testing the evidence is weaker: Holm over all 28 gain tests gives 0.13 (Zambia pooled) and 0.07 (Zambia phone A); over the 7 HeAR tests alone it gives 0.029 and 0.018.
* Mean over the two external countries (table_e6_summary.csv): gain 0.000 (MFCC), -0.003 (spectrogram), -0.003 (VGGish), +0.035 (HeAR). Mean AUROC with the cough 0.717 for HeAR. Worst country: Zambia for MFCC (-0.005) and spectrogram (-0.016), Kenya for VGGish (-0.009). Mean AUROC with the cough is 0.682, 0.679 and 0.679 for the first three, against 0.682 for the questions alone and 0.717 for HeAR.
* Cough alone is close to chance for MFCC, spectrogram and VGGish: Zambia (pooled) 0.49 to 0.58, Kenya 0.57 to 0.58, with intervals that include 0.5 in 14 of the 15 test-set and representation combinations (e6_results.csv). HeAR is higher: 0.719 on Zambia (pooled; 0.69 to 0.73 per phone) and 0.633 on Kenya.
* The frozen CODA threshold does not carry over. Sensitivity stays near the target in Zambia (0.84 to 0.98) but specificity is low (0.14 to 0.37; with HeAR 0.21 to 0.29 against 0.36 for the questions alone at sensitivity 0.84, so the two are not at the same operating point). In Kenya almost everyone is flagged (sensitivity 0.96 to 0.99, specificity 0.09 to 0.18; HeAR 0.12).
* Zambia to Kenya and Kenya to Zambia with the new 0.5 s clips: the Zambia to Kenya gain stays about zero (-0.011 to +0.002). The Kenya to Zambia gain is +0.028 (0.007 to 0.049, p = 0.009) for MFCC and +0.027 (0.009 to 0.045, p = 0.003) for the spectrogram summary, and +0.013 (-0.020 to 0.047) for VGGish. HeAR gives -0.008 (Zambia to Kenya, p = 0.22) and +0.001 (Kenya to Zambia, p = 0.85), so HeAR does not share the Kenya to Zambia gain; its cough-only AUROC in that direction is 0.503. The old results had gains of about -0.016 and -0.004.
* Nine-country probe (country from one cough, unseen people): accuracy 0.83 (MFCC), 0.81 (spectrogram), 0.88 (VGGish), 0.955 (HeAR), against 0.11 by chance; macro AUROC 0.976 to 0.983 for the first three and 0.997 for HeAR. Kenya and Zambia are identified almost perfectly (recall 0.97 to 0.98 with VGGish, 0.99 with HeAR). The confusions are among South Africa, Tanzania and Uganda, and between Madagascar and Zambia or Uganda; HeAR makes far fewer of them.

## Do the earlier findings hold in these countries?
* F4 (the gain disappears in a new country): holds for MFCC, spectrogram and VGGish (every CODA-trained gain within about 0.02, no interval excludes zero). It does not hold for HeAR: +0.031 on Zambia and +0.038 on Kenya, see above. This matches Experiment 4, where HeAR was the only encoder with a positive pooled gain.
* F5 (cough alone is close to chance in a new country): holds for the first three (0.49 to 0.58 here against 0.51 to 0.55 averaged over CODA countries). HeAR is above it on Zambia (0.72) and moderately so on Kenya (0.63).
* F6 (recording context is easier to read than TB): holds. Country is recognised with macro AUROC of about 0.98 (0.997 with HeAR), while cough alone for TB is about 0.55 (0.72 with HeAR on Zambia).
* F10 (thresholds do not travel): holds, with the same pattern as in CODA: the target sensitivity is roughly kept and specificity collapses, most strongly in Kenya.
* F7 (country-only score) cannot be tested here, because each test set is a single country.
* F9 (some answers mean different things in different places) was not tested directly. The roadmap reports that in Kenya HIV and previous TB are more common among people without TB, the reverse of the CODA direction. A CODA-trained questions model would then be pushed the wrong way on Kenya, which may be part of why its AUROC there is only 0.668. We did not check this in our 118 people.

## Why did you choose this configuration?
Fixed before any result (HeAR was added later, with the clip preparation fixed in Notebook 05 on CODA's random split, not on Zambia or Kenya): Kenya phone only (CODA and Zambia are phone recordings), 10 coughs per person, Zambia scored per phone and pooled over the same people, identical 0.5 s clips for all three datasets, threshold and models as in Notebook 04. No test label was used for any choice. The 21 Kenya people were left out because their audio is missing, not because of their labels; their labels were looked at only afterwards, as a check, and did not change the decision.

## Were these the intended results?
Mostly yes. Earlier new-country results already showed gains close to zero (Zambia to Kenya -0.016, Kenya to Zambia -0.003, CODA leave-one-country-out pooled +0.008), and CODA-trained models tested on Zambia and Kenya agree. Cough alone is also far below what a model trained inside the same dataset reaches (about 0.74 for fine-tuned VGGish on Zambia in Notebook 01, a different model, against 0.49 to 0.58 here). Two things differ from before. With the new identical 0.5 s clips, cough-only AUROC in the Zambia to Kenya and Kenya to Zambia transfers rose (0.55 to 0.62 and 0.585 to 0.61 to 0.68). And the Kenya to Zambia gain became positive (+0.027 to +0.028 for MFCC and spectrogram, intervals above zero), which we did not expect. HeAR was also unexpected: a positive gain in a new country, which Experiment 4 had only shown within a country-held-out setting at +0.014.

## Why do you think you got these results?
Working explanation; none of this was tested in this experiment.
* The nine-country probe shows that a single cough reveals its country with 81 to 88 % accuracy, so a large part of what audio features carry is country, device and setting. A TB model trained in one country can learn that signature, and it does not carry over to a new country. This fits cough-only AUROC near 0.5 and the zero gains.
* The frozen threshold fails for the same kind of reason: scores shift with loudness and recording channel (median clip level about -19 dB in CODA, -21 dB in Zambia, -25 dB in Kenya) and, in Kenya, with a TB share of 71 %, so a threshold fixed on CODA does not give the target operating point.
* HeAR, working explanation (not tested): it is trained on health sounds, so its 512 numbers may carry cough features the other three summaries lose. It is also the strongest country fingerprint (macro AUROC 0.997), so it carries a lot of recording information as well. A gain in a country the model never saw argues against a pure country shortcut, but we have not ruled out that it reads something that happens to correlate with TB in Zambia and Kenya. It should be repeated on a second dataset before it is reported as a TB signal.
* The higher cough-only AUROC with the new clips is probably due to the identical clipping: the old Zambia clips were padded with silence, and a length or silence difference between the two datasets no longer separates people.
* The Kenya to Zambia gain is not explained. Possible causes: the two datasets share a recording-setting effect that a Kenya-trained model picks up and Zambia rewards, a real but small cough signal, or chance given the number of comparisons (about 6 transfer tests, uncorrected; only the spectrogram result stays below 0.05 divided by 6). The follow-up checks of Notebook 06b are reported in the next section.

## Follow-up checks on Kenya to Zambia (Notebook 06b)
Three checks on the MFCC, spectrogram and VGGish gains, rules fixed before running (files kz_*.csv).
* Different draws of Kenya coughs (10 draws of up to 10 coughs per person from a pool of up to 30): the gain was above zero in 10 of 10 draws for all three representations; mean +0.023 (MFCC, range 0.009 to 0.034), +0.027 (spectrogram, 0.016 to 0.036), +0.021 (VGGish, 0.003 to 0.036). The interval excluded zero in 4, 10 and 3 of 10 draws.
* Per Zambian phone and site: pooled +0.028, +0.027, +0.013 (MFCC, spectrogram, VGGish). Positive on all three phones for MFCC and spectrogram (phone A +0.034, B +0.019 to +0.024, C +0.029; VGGish phone A -0.004 and C -0.008). Kanyama (215 people, 47 TB) +0.032 to +0.038; Chawama (110 people, 10 TB) -0.009 (MFCC, spectrogram) and -0.074 (VGGish), with too few TB cases to judge.
* Label-shuffled control (cough part trained on shuffled labels, 100 runs): shuffled gains average about 0 with a 95th percentile of 0.012 to 0.018. Empirical p = 0.02 (MFCC), 0.01 (spectrogram), 0.109 (VGGish).
* Verdict by the rules fixed in advance: MFCC and spectrogram supported, VGGish not. The same 325 Zambian people are used in every check, so they are not independent; the result stood out among 21 tests (Holm p 0.059 spectrogram, 0.18 MFCC) and goes one direction only (Kenya to Zambia, not Zambia to Kenya). The mechanism is unknown. Two untested ideas: Kenya's non-TB people are mostly other respiratory patients, so the model learns something closer to TB versus other disease; or training inside one country removes the country shortcut. HeAR does not show this gain.

## Other things to know
* Question definitions differ across datasets. Smoking means "smoked in the last week" in CODA and "ever smoked" in the other two. Unknown HIV status and "not sure" for previous TB are treated as missing. Cough duration is converted to days in each dataset (Kenya: weeks times 7).
* The old numbers in Table E6b come from Notebook 03 and their representation is not confirmed (marked `unconfirmed` in e6_results.csv).
* Optional step 8 (train on Zambia and Kenya, test on each CODA country) was not run.

## Limitations to state
* Kenya is case-control (71 % TB in the retained people): compare gains and thresholds, not absolute AUROC.
* Small test sets give wide intervals (Zambia has 57 TB cases, Kenya 118 people).
* The Kenya test set here (118 people) differs from the one in Notebook 03 (stored features, includes the 21 people without audio), and the Zambia cohort here (325) differs from the paired cohort (299). The old versus new comparison is therefore indicative, not like for like.
* Many comparisons were made (4 representations, 5 test sets, 2 transfer directions; 28 gain tests); the p-values in e6_results.csv are not corrected. Holm-corrected values: Zambia pooled HeAR 0.13 (28 tests) or 0.029 (7 HeAR tests).
* CODA, Zambia and Kenya differ in recording device, setting and loudness.
